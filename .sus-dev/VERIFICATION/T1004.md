## The claim checker

`pnpm claims` runs third in `pnpm verify`, after handoff and reachability. It scans the
**claim-bearing** surface — `README.md`, `docs/{LIMITATIONS,MAINTENANCE,SUPPORT_MATRIX,COVERAGE_MATRIX}.md`,
`skill/SKILL.md` — and blocks:

- **comparative claims** (`beats`, `superior`, `faster than`, `better than`, `outperforms`,
  `more effective than`) while the benchmark gate is closed. The gate is read from
  `evals/benchmarks/results/status.json`, the same artefact `pnpm benchmark` writes, so
  there is no second source of truth;
- **absolute claims** (`guarantees`, `100 % accessible`, `proven to`, `never fails`,
  `always accessible`) always, because no benchmark makes those true.

Two exemptions keep it honest rather than merely strict: fenced and inline code (a protocol
must be able to quote the words it forbids), and negated statements — `does not guarantee`
is the limitation, not the overstatement. Both are pinned by tests, because a checker that
blocks honest limitation statements drives authors to delete the limitation instead.

It immediately caught three real defects: a naked `superior` in the README table, a
`guarantee` in the maintenance page, and — the useful one — that `docs/LIMITATIONS.md` and
`docs/MAINTENANCE.md` did not exist at all.

## Honest limitations

- The checker is a regex over prose. It catches the words in its list and nothing else: a
  claim phrased around them ("clearly ahead of the baseline") passes. It is a floor, not a
  semantic reviewer, and the audit is what covers the rest.
- The exception list for documents that *discuss* claims is deliberately small but is still
  an allowlist; a new protocol document gets added by hand.
- `docs/SUPPORT_MATRIX.md` is generated and drift-checked, so its content is only as honest
  as the smoke receipts behind it — and today that means one verified host and three
  untested ones.
