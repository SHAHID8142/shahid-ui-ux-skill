## What "complete matrix" now means, mechanically

The playbook contract gained `variantMatrix`: for every variant, every state the
machine lists must be declared **enabled or refused with a reason**, the union
must be exact, a row may not appear for an undeclared variant, and a declared
variant may not go without a row. Eleven of the ten playbooks use it to refuse
states that would otherwise be inherited by accident — a ghost button has no
`success` (nothing to confirm), an info dialog has no `error` (no request to
fail), an error toast cannot confirm success. Each refusal carries a reason ≥8
characters, because "not applicable" is a shrug and a shrug is how matrices rot.

Motion: 64 transitions across the ten components, every one with an explicit
motion spec, an interruption rule, a rapid-toggle rule, and a reduced-motion
equivalent that keeps the information and the operation. `motion: none` appears
23 times — all on sync-feedback paths where an animation would *delay* the only
feedback that matters (error text, label swap, navigation) — and the schema
refuses `none` without a reason. Components with `loading` must declare a
stale-response rule; all four do.

Forward compatibility: the machine embedded in each playbook is validated by the
same `sus.state-machine` contract T0600 owns, and states are drawn only from its
vocabulary, so tightening T0600 fails here rather than drifting.

## Honest limitations

- **Provenance is thin and it shows.** The provenance catalog holds three claim
  ids, so only the `*baseline-floor` rules are `authoritative`; the rest are
  marked `convention` (project decisions, explicitly not evidence-backed). That
  is honest but it is also a ceiling on how much these playbooks can claim. It is
  a T0301 research gap, not something I can author around.
- Rules are decision procedures, not measurements: nothing here proves the built
  component is good, only that the decisions that must be made were named. The
  binding to reality is T0708 (full loop on fixture) and T0906.
- I authored 64 transitions by hand. The schema proves they are declared and
  reachable; it cannot prove they are the *right* transitions for these
  components. Review against real components belongs to the audit phase.
- I hit three real schema constraints while authoring (terminal states need no
  outbound; `rapidToggle` has no "ignore" value; matrix rows must be exhaustive)
  and fixed the content, not the contract — except where the contract was
  genuinely incomplete (the variant matrix itself).
